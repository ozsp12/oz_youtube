Slide 1: Presenting the problem (53s)
We will analyze one of the most well-known problems in classical mechanics, the sliding block on a sliding wedge. The block has mass lowercase m, and the wedge has mass uppercase M. The wedge rests on a smooth, horizontal surface, and it can slide freely without any resistance. The surface of the wedge is inclined at an angle alpha with respect to the horizontal and presents no static or kinetic friction with the block. We place the block right on top of the wedge, and then let the system go.

In this setup, even though the block slides downward, the wedge itself will also begin to slide to the left, as a reaction to the block’s motion. The motion is coupled, and there is a holonomic constraint, as the block does not lose contact with the wedge's hypotenuse. Our goal will be to analyze this interaction and determine the accelerations of both the block and the wedge using Newtonian mechanics.

To properly analyze this system, we must be precise about the frame of reference from which observations are made. At the bottom of the image, we see observer O sub 1, who is fixed with respect to the ground. This observer defines an inertial reference frame, one in which Newton’s laws apply in their standard form, without correction of inertial forces. 

At the top of the wedge lies observer O sub 2, who moves along with the inclined plane. This observer is attached to a non-inertial reference frame, which itself accelerates horizontally as the wedge slides. From the perspective of observer O sub 2, the block appears to accelerate down the inclined plane of the wedge. 

Still, this motion is influenced not only by gravity but also by the geometrical constraints of the system. Throughout our solution, we shall adopt the perspective of observer O sub 1, working strictly within the inertial frame and relying on Newtonian forces alone.
