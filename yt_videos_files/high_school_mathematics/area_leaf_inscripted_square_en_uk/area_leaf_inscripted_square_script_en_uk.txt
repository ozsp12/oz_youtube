In this vídeo, we will calculate the area of the leaf inscribed in a square of side A. It is part of the challenge to understand how this leaf is created. Inside the square, two quarter-circles are drawn. One centered at the point D at the lower right corner and the other centered at the point B at the upper left corner. The shaded region represents the overlapping area enclosed by the arcs. Our goal is to determine the exact area of this lens-shaped region, which we will denote as the area of figure A E C F A.


Slide #2: Construction of the Circles (35s)
To better understand the geometry, let us extend the quarter-circles beyond the square. The first circle, labeled S sub one, is centered at point B, at the upper left corner of the square. The second circle, labeled S sub two, is centered at point D, at the lower right corner. Both circles have the same radius, equal to the side of the square, denoted by the letter A. These two circles intersect and create the arcs labeled E for circle S sub one and arc F for circle S sub 2, forming the lens-shaped region we observed inside the square. 


Slide #3: Area of the Quarter-Circle A-E-C-D-A (13s)
To solve this problem, let us now compute the area of one of the quarter-circles denoted by the figure A E C D A. This is just the area of a quarter of a circle, so equal to pi times A squared divided by four.


Slide #4: Area of the Triangle A-C-D (13s)
Now, let us compute the area of the right triangle A C D. Its base and height are both equal to the side of the square, denoted by the letter A. Therefore, its area is equal to A squared divided by two.

Slide #6: Relation Between the Areas (29s)
The upper half of the leaf, labeled A E C A, can be expressed as the difference between the quarter-circle A E C D A and the right triangle A C D. Therefore, the area of A E C A equals the area of the quarter-circle minus the area of the triangle. Since this upper region represents half of the entire leaf, we obtain that one-half of the total area of A E C F A is equal to the area of the quarter-circle minus the area of the triangle.


Slide #7: Final Expression for the Area (15s)
Substituting the known expressions for the areas of the quarter-circle and the triangle, we obtain that the area of the lens-shaped region A E C F A is equal to A squared divided by two, multiplied by the quantity pi minus two.
