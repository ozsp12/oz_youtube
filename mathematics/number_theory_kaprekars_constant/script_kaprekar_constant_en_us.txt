Dattatreya Ramchandra Kaprekar was an Indian recreational mathematician who described several classes of natural numbers, including the Kaprekar, Harshad, and self numbers, and discovered Kaprekar's constant, named after him.

In this video, we will present Kaprekar's constant, the number 6174, which is a fixed point of Kaprekar's routine, an iterative algorithm. Any four-digit number in base 10 with at least two distinct digits will reach 6174 within seven iterations. 

The algorithm runs on any natural number in any given number base. Each iteration of Kaprekar's routine begins with a four-digit integer number, sorts the digits into both descending and ascending order, and calculates the difference between the two resulting numbers. 

This is the algorithm:
Step 1: Select any four-digit number that has at least two different digits; leading zeros are allowed.
Step 2: Create two new four-digit numbers by arranging the original digits in ascending and descending order, adding leading zeros if necessary.
Step 3: Subtract the smaller number from the bigger number.
Step 4: If the result is not 6174, return to step 2 and repeat the process.

Take, for example, the numbers 2091, 3524, and 1129. Using Kaprekar's routine, they all eventually reach Kaprekar's constant, 6174, as they should.
